.In our trials, July 2026
****
We instantiated this chapter's two scenarios as a reproducible experiment: a
synthetic trading-desk warehouse seeded with exactly the ambiguities described
here — the deprecated order-level strategy tag, order-time versus fill-time
grain, notional versus mark-to-market versus realized P&L, a 30-September
fiscal year, bitemporal counterparty tiers with backdated corrections, and a
`volume` column holding notional — plus 44 pre-registered questions with
handwritten gold SQL, scored by execution accuracy. Three models (GPT-5.6-terra,
Claude Sonnet 5, Gemini 2.5 Flash; temperature 0, three runs each, modal verdict).

On the 24 trap questions, the two frontier models under Scenario A's context
(schema plus sample rows) scored 71 percent — and under Scenario B's (declared
semantics plus the domain essay, at roughly the same token count) went to
**100 percent** [Wilson 95%: 86–100]. The mid-tier model went from 63 to 71
percent, and — a result this chapter predicted — *lost* accuracy when the essay
was added to its context, over-contextualization degrading it as surely as
starvation. Control questions with no seeded trap passed at 90–100 percent in
every arm: the schema was answerable; the semantics were the difference.
Harness, data generator, and every prompt and completion:
github.com/thegoodtailor/semantic-enterprise-experiments. One hostile schema,
one date, three named models — a demonstration of the mechanism, not an
industry benchmark.
****

.Grounding, measured — our trials, July 2026
****
Published evaluations put semantic-layer accuracy at or near the ceiling (dbt
Labs 2026: 98–100 percent; AtScale: 92.5 versus 20 percent raw; Snowflake
Cortex Analyst: ~90 versus a 16 percent raw baseline) and raw text-to-SQL
against enterprise-grade schemas near the floor (Spider 2.0 at release: 21.3
percent). In our own pre-registered trials (44 questions, six seeded
ambiguities, three models — full method and harness at
github.com/thegoodtailor/semantic-enterprise-experiments), frontier models
scored 67–71 percent on trap questions from the raw schema and **100 percent**
through the governed semantic context; with structure alone (declared joins but
definitions stripped), they fell to 75–83 percent — the meaning, not the
structure, carries the accuracy. Where the raw arms failed, the modal
foil-matched error was strategy-tag selection — the deprecated column — exactly
the failure class the declared-joins doctrine predicts. Numbers are dated;
the mechanism is the claim.
****
