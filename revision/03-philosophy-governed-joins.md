# Brief 03 — The Governed-Joins Doctrine (Stage 2, core)

Supersedes review §1.2's framing and revises the book's flat-Gold absolutism into the sharper, defensible, *original* claim. This is Iman's observation, now fully evidenced. All sources verified June 2026.

## 1. The doctrine, stated

The book currently argues "flatten Gold, joins are the enemy." The evidence says something more precise, and it is a better thesis because no one else has named it:

> **The cost and the risk were never the join. They are join *inference*.**
>
> For most traditional business interrogation, best bang-for-buck and semantic integrity come from two governed surfaces over one vocabulary. **The smorgasbord:** flat, richly documented Gold marts — a broad curated menu of pick-and-mix assets covering the head of the question distribution — cheapest tokens, zero join risk, instant answers. **The explore:** governed join graphs for the tail — the rabbit holes — where the joins, grains, and aggregation rules are *declared once* in the semantic layer, the engine generates SQL deterministically, and the AI sits **above** the join: it picks fields, filters, pivots. It never plans a join path, so it cannot hallucinate one.
>
> The agent picks; the engine joins.

This dissolves the apparent tension the book keeps fighting (flat vs dimensional) into a single law — **joins are declared, never inferred** — and it is *strictly stronger* than "flatten everything": flat marts are the degenerate case (zero joins to declare), explores are the general case (joins declared with integrity machinery), and both forbid the same thing: the model writing a join from guesswork.

## 2. The evidence (every plank sourced)

**Join inference is the dominant text-to-SQL failure class.** DIN-SQL's error taxonomy on Spider found schema-linking the largest failure category even with a dedicated module (arXiv 2304.11015); SEA-SQL's breakdown on BIRD: schema-linking 37% + JOIN errors 36% ≈ **73% of all errors are mapping questions to tables/columns/join-paths** (arXiv 2408.04919 — quote-check Fig. 6 before print); the largest independent error study (12,340 queries): incorrect table selection is the top semantic error, ON-clause and bridge-table misjoins recurrent, and self-repair loops fix 11–23% of errors while *introducing* 5–40% more at 1–3.8x latency (arXiv 2501.09310); Spider 2.0: enterprise databases "often lack explicit foreign keys, forcing models to infer potential keys… leading to errors" (arXiv 2411.07763). The model's join is a guess; the guess is the failure class.

**Above declared joins, accuracy goes near-ceiling.** AtScale: TPC-DS across **two dozen physical tables**, LLM sees one virtual flat table, engine resolves joins → 92.5% vs 20% raw-schema-with-FKs; their own words: "removing the need for the LLM to infer joins" (atscale.com/blog/enable-natural-language-prompting-with-semantic-layer-genai). dbt 2026: "the LLM can't produce an incorrect join or a bad aggregation" — SL 100% in scope vs 64.5% text-to-SQL (docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026). Snowflake Cortex Analyst's join support is *declaration-only by design* ("the expanded capability only works if the relationships are defined") with explicit fan-trap/chasm-trap validation, 92% on join scenarios vs 55% GPT-4o single-shot (snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-joins-star-snowflake-schemas). Google on Looker Conversational Analytics: "Since the agent isn't writing the whole SQL query, it doesn't need to 'understand' the data and can operate more accurately and deterministically" (cloud.google.com/blog/products/business-intelligence/a-closer-look-at-looker-conversational-analytics). Strategy/Mosaic, the sentence the chapter should hang on: **"It cannot produce a wrong join because it never writes a join"** — and both of its raw-SQL baseline failures were exactly fan-out and bridge-misselection, producing an 8x-inflated dollar figure that "executed successfully and returned a number" (strategy.com/software/ai-token-cost-and-accuracy-benchmark).

**The integrity machinery exists and has names.** This is what "careful means to join" means concretely, and the book should teach it: **Looker symmetric aggregates** (hash-PK + SUM DISTINCT making measures correct across fan-out; on by default per explore; depends on declared PKs and relationship cardinality — docs.cloud.google.com/looker/docs/best-practices/understanding-symmetric-aggregates); **Malloy aggregate locality** ("aggregate calculations navigate this graph… always computed correctly regardless of join pattern, avoiding the fan and chasm traps" — docs.malloydata.dev/documentation/language/join.html); **MetricFlow entity-graph join resolution** ("avoids fan-out or chasm joins… based on the entity types"); **Cortex Analyst join validation** (per-CTE double-counting checks). Four independent engines, one principle: correctness of aggregation over joins is a *property of the declared model*, not of the query author. Lloyd Tabb, the man who built Looker, states the whole philosophy in one line: "The act of querying becomes simply picking dimensions and measures… it becomes much harder to get incorrect results" (lloydtabb.substack.com/p/querying-a-semantic-data-model).

**The rabbit holes are cost-engineered.** Looker **aggregate awareness** routes a query to the smallest rollup that can serve its grain and falls back to the join graph only when granularity demands — pre-materialized heads and explorable tails behind *one* logical surface (docs.cloud.google.com/looker/docs/aggregate_awareness). Token economics: Strategy measured 50% input-token reduction (semantic context vs raw schema) rising to ~98% in the no-LLM-SQL architecture; their scale illustration: ~$15,500/month of schema-context overhead for raw text-to-SQL vs ~$450 through the governed engine. (Honesty clause: vendor numbers, and cost is partly *shifted* into platform licensing — say so.)

**The two-surface pattern is the converging industry consensus.** dbt's official guidance is literally "both, for different things" — SL for accuracy-critical, exploration falls back with as much context as possible; phData's sizing rule for semantic views: "start with the 20% of data that answers 80% of questions"; Omni's three-layer promotion (workbook → governed model → materialized schema) frames materialization as the *last* step driven by observed consumption; Looker hub-and-spoke does governance-center/domain-edge. The smorgasbord serves the head; the explore serves the tail; materialization is a routing/cost decision inside one governed surface — not dogma.

## 3. The honest pressure points (incorporate, don't bury)

1. **Coverage is the tax.** In dbt's benchmark the >2-hop questions scored SL **0%** while raw GPT-5.3 scored 100% on the same questions; MetricFlow caps at two hops. The governed surface fails *closed* — an error, not a wrong number — which is the right failure but still a failure. This plugs directly into the book's existing Gap-as-Signal / Extension-Intake patterns: the tail beyond the explore's reach is the intake queue, not a bypass warrant.
2. **The raw gap is closing** (32.7% → 64.5% in two years; ~90% on well-modeled data). The durable claim is therefore *determinism and variance*, not a snapshot of model weakness: an answer that is 100%-by-construction in scope, with provenance, survives audit; a 90%-on-a-good-day answer does not. Write it that way and the thesis cannot age out.
3. **Simple queries don't need the machinery** (Strategy's raw baseline went 15/17). True — and the failures concentrate precisely in the multi-fact, fan-out, bridge-table questions, i.e., the financially material ones. The 88% that works is what makes the 12% lethal: it earns trust the wrong answers then spend.
4. **dbt's own modeling guidance prefers normalized marts under the SL** ("the more you pre-compute and freeze, the less flexible") — the one credible in-camp dissent from the smorgasbord plank. Answer: freezing is exactly right for the *head* (stability, cheapness, auditability of named published assets) and wrong for the *tail* (which is why the explore exists). Question-distribution, not dogma.

## 4. The battle map (replaces "Where Serious People Disagree" §star-schemas; fixes review §1.4)

Frame as named camps, fought on the record:

- **Our camp — "Lifecycle, not model; joins declared, never inferred."** Allies on the record: **Joe Reis** — "The Medallion architecture is no more a data model than a parking lot is a type of car… Gold is simply business-ready data… Star Schema, wide tables (OBT), 3NF, Data Vault, or any other way you see fit" (practicaldatamodeling.substack.com/p/medallion-architecture-is-not-a-data) — medallion prescribes nothing, so placement of discipline is *ours to argue*, not heresy. **Fivetran/Kaminsky** — the OBT benchmark author whose own conclusion is dimensional staging upstream, flat consumption downstream, i.e., our architecture (fivetran.com/blog/star-schema-vs-obt). **Tabb/Malloy**, and the entire AI-analytics vendor convergence (Genie: "pre-join or de-normalize… five or fewer tables"; AtScale's virtual flat table; Looker explores). Handle Reis precisely: ally on lifecycle/model orthogonality, *and* the source of the "dismissing Kimball is dangerously ignorant" warning — we agree, which is why the discipline lives in Silver and the war is only about who plans joins.
- **The Databricks/Microsoft orthodoxy:** 3NF/Vault Silver, physical Kimball stars in Gold ("We see a lot of Kimball style star schema-based data models… fit in this Gold Layer" — databricks.com/glossary/medallion-architecture). Counter: a physical star at consumption duplicates structure the semantic layer already encodes logically, and hands the join back to whoever queries below the layer; note their own docs' Gold examples are flat aggregates — the orthodoxy is soft at its center.
- **The Snowflake position:** keep physical stars, put semantic views over them ("We recommend starting with a simple star schema"). The closest rival — it already concedes the main point (AI above declared joins) and differs only on physical layout beneath the layer. Counter: once the layer is the sole interface, physical shape is an engine/cost decision; columnar economics plus rollup routing make flat-head + Silver-direct-tail the better default; the Fivetran data shows the performance argument for stars is mixed at best.
- **The anti-modeling camp:** "long-context models will read the whole schema" (the "Death of Schema Linking" line, arXiv 2408.07702, plus the raw text-to-SQL trajectory). Counter: Spider 2.0 at 755+ columns without FKs is where this dies; variance and auditability don't improve with context length; and the camp's own best results come on *well-modeled* data.
- **The academic nuance:** normalization study (arXiv 2510.01989) — denormalized tables hurt LLM aggregation accuracy via duplication/NULLs. Read correctly, this is *our* evidence: raw OBT and raw stars are both unsafe for agent-written aggregates; the fix is measures declared at grain with fan-out-proof machinery — the explore.

## 5. Fixing the Silver contradiction (review §1.4c) — the doctrine does it for free

Resolve with one precise statement, replacing both the Ch3 equivocation and the ~line 822 anecdote's muddle: **Silver's discipline is logical — grain, SCD2, conformance, declared relationships — and its physical shape is an engine decision.** Wide entity-canonical tables per domain are the default physical form; the *join graph over them* is declared in the semantic layer and exposed as explores (Silver-direct). What the engineer in the anecdote got wrong was not "building dimensions" — it was **re-materializing, in Gold, structure that was already declared logically**, forcing every consumer below the layer to re-plan joins the model had already settled. Rewrite the anecdote to that point and the contradiction disappears; Decoupling (Position 1) finally does the work it was hired for.
